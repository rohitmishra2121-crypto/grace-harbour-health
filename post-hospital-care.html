The Critical Recovery Window

Why the First 72 Hours After Discharge Matter Most

Medical studies show that nearly 20% of discharged seniors face adverse events or emergency room readmissions within 30 days. The root causes are almost always non-clinical: medication mix-ups, unassisted falls on the way to the bathroom, dehydration, or exhaustion. Having structured support in place prevents these setbacks.

Medication Confusion

Discharge instructions frequently alter prescriptions. Caregivers ensure timely reminders according to the new schedule.

High Fall Risk

Anesthesia fog and muscle atrophy make the first transfers treacherous. Steady physical assistance mitigates devastating falls.

Malnutrition & Fluids

Seniors lack the stamina to stand and cook. Fresh healing meals and consistent hydration speed wound closure and recovery.

Red-Flag Vigilance

Early detection of infection, excessive swelling, or shortness of breath allows prompt intervention before an ER visit is necessary.

Structured Recovery Roadmap

The 3-Phase Roadmap to Full Independence.

Recovery isn’t all or nothing. Our flexible plans scale down as your loved one regains strength, so you never pay for care hours you no longer need.

Phase 1: Days 1–3

Settle-In & Stabilization

Intensive, hands-on support immediately following hospital discharge. Focus on pain regimen compliance, safe commode transfers, home stabilization, and preventing falls.

Typical Schedule: 8–12 hrs/day or 24/7 overnight
Phase 2: Weeks 1–2

Strengthening & Routine

Building stamina and mobility. Caregivers assist with daily walking intervals, prescribed physical therapy exercises, nutritious home meals, and personal grooming.

Typical Schedule: 4–6 hrs/day
Phase 3: Weeks 3–4

Transition to Independence

Stepping down support as self-sufficiency returns. Light housekeeping, grocery runs, driving to follow-up surgical clinics, or transition to light companionship.

Typical Schedule: 3 hrs, 2–3 times/week

Discharge Questions

Frequently Asked Questions About Post-Hospital Care

Everything you need to know about planning a seamless hospital-to-home transition in Nova Scotia.

When should we arrange post-hospital care before discharge?

We recommend contacting us 24 to 48 hours prior to your anticipated discharge from the QEII Health Sciences Centre, Dartmouth General Hospital, or local surgical centers. This gives us adequate time to liaise with your hospital discharge planner, review restrictions, and ensure a dedicated caregiver is waiting when you arrive home.

Can your caregiver meet us at the hospital on discharge day?

Yes. If family members are unable to take time off work, our caregiver can meet your parent at the hospital discharge area, carry discharge paperwork and medication bags, assist with the drive or taxi, and ensure your loved one is safely settled in bed with water and lunch.

Do you collaborate with VON Nova Scotia nurses or physical therapists?

Yes, absolutely. VON home nursing visits typically last 30 to 45 minutes for wound care or catheter checks. Grace Harbour caregivers provide the vital hours in between—handling meals, bathroom transfers, hygiene, and encouraging the exercises prescribed by your visiting physiotherapist.

What if my parent needs overnight support or someone beside them 24/7?

We offer both awake overnight care and 24/7 live-in recovery support. During the first critical 48 to 72 hours following major surgery, having an awake caregiver on hand ensures safe nighttime commode transfers and eliminates the fear of falling in the dark.

Are there long-term contracts if we only need help for 2 or 3 weeks?

No. We never lock families into long-term contracts. Post-hospital recovery care is designed to be short-term and flexible. You can adjust or discontinue service at any time with straightforward 24-hour notice as your parent recovers their independence.
